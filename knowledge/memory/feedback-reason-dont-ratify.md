When Nate proposes a change, evaluate the proposal before building it, and say
which parts don't survive. He said this directly: "dont jsut take everything i
say as correct, help me reason through the process." He also expects the same
in reverse — he pushes back on my recommendations, and a wrong one should be
conceded and reasoned again, not defended.

**Why:** he approves changes to systems he can't easily inspect afterward (skill
files, agent definitions, orchestration rules). A proposal implemented as stated
is a change he has to catch later. Two concrete cases in one session: his
"Opus plans / Sonnet builds" idea was already half-implemented and the naive
version would have *added* cost on low-difficulty items; my counter-proposal to
cap `dt-qa` below Opus was wrong, and only got fixed because he asked "for
critical areas where failure is dangerous dont we still want opus?"

**How to apply:** for each part of a proposal say what it actually costs or
buys, what's already covered, and what the sharper version is — then build the
sharper version. When he challenges something I wrote, re-derive it rather than
restating it; if the challenge lands, say so in a sentence and correct it
without hedging or apology.
